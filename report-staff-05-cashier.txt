8. HYPOTHESIS UNDER TEST (from the test brief)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Severe database deadlocks caused by concurrent cashier processing threads
  attempting to write to and lock the global revenue ledger tables out of
  order.

Status: NOT OBSERVED and NOT EXERCISED. Contradicted by the code.

Evidence from the backend source:
- Contradicted: there is no revenue ledger table. The payment models are
  StatementOfAccount, SOAItem and Payment (payments/models.py), and a payment
  is one row per application.

- Contradicted: receive_payment takes no explicit locks and does not run in a
  transaction (payments/views.py:462-534). It writes one Payment row, one
  status log and one notification, each committed on its own. With no multi-
  row lock ordering there is no deadlock pattern.

- The real concurrency risk is the opposite, a missing lock. Two cashiers
  confirming the same application at once can both pass the "already paid"
  check (views.py:475) before either saves. The later OR number overwrites the
  earlier one, and two status logs and two applicant notifications are
  written. or_number has no unique constraint (payments/models.py:52).

- Related: payment_reports recomputes a SUM and four GROUP BY counts on every
  request (payments/views.py:874-895), and receipt_document rebuilds the
  receipt spreadsheet on every call (views.py:570).

- To test the race: run this script with -e MUTATE=1 against a disposable
  pending payment and count the status log rows afterwards.
